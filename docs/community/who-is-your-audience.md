# Who Is Your Audience

This is a question Silverware Games gets a lot.

## Audience Is Not Separate

The usual version of the question assumes something like this: the game is for the audience, not for the creative team. The team’s personal taste is treated as separate from the product, or even as an obstacle to it.

That is not how we work.

When Silverware Games makes something, we want to enjoy it ourselves. If we are making a game with a younger target audience in mind, we still want the people making it to genuinely like what they are building. We do not believe the best work comes from standing outside the project and cynically guessing what a demographic will respond to.

## The Risk of Pandering

Taken to its extreme, that mindset becomes pure pandering. You could poll 1,000 people, collect preferences, follow the consensus at every step, and optimize the product around that data. But at that point, an important question appears:

Why does the creative team need to care about the work at all?

If every decision is outsourced to market signals, then the work can quickly lose taste, conviction, and personality.

There is also a practical problem. Once a team is designing for an audience they do not meaningfully relate to, they often lose their own sense of what is good. Instead of making strong choices, they start making cynical guesses:

“Kids like bright colors, so make everything louder.”
“This audience likes fast rewards, so remove the quiet moments.”
“This group won’t care about quality here, so lower the standard.”

That approach can produce something engineered to appeal in theory while feeling hollow in practice.

At the same time, the opposite mistake is real too. If we only make something we enjoy and nobody else connects with it, that is also a failure.

## The Real Goal Is Overlap

So the real goal is not self-indulgence, and it is not pandering.

The goal is the overlap:

- What we like
- What we want to make
- What audiences like

That is the sweet spot.

Finding that overlap has been an ongoing undertaking for us. Sometimes the people who connect with our style feel rare. Sometimes moving toward "what the market wants" can make the work worse instead of better. But over time, we have found that the strongest path is not to abandon our taste. It is to refine it, express it clearly, and build for the people who will genuinely respond to it.

## Our Audience Definition

In other words:

Our audience is not an abstract demographic first. It is the group of people who will love the thing we honestly love making.

That is the audience Silverware Games is trying to reach.
