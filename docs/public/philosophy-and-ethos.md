# Philosophy and Ethos

Our goal is simple: the entire process of making art should be joyful — from buying the materials, to the first pen stroke, to finishing the work, to sharing it with others.

If a society stops producing joyful art, it may be a warning sign that something deeper is wrong.

## Our Values

### Fun
We create lighthearted, amusing games that people can easily enjoy. Sometimes the world makes it hard to see the fun in everyday life — we want our work to help people rediscover it.

### Imagination
Our games explore unusual settings, unexpected mechanics, and playful characters. We enjoy blending genres and inventing new kinds of play.

### People
Human connection makes the world go round. We create content to share with others because growing and supporting our community is one of our goals. **A game isn't truly a game if no one plays it.** That’s why we stay connected with our players and community.

## The Eternal Struggle
Like any creative studio, we constantly balance competing ideas about art, design, and creativity.

### Fun vs Professional
Creative work lives in tension between playfulness and professionalism.

We take our work seriously — but we try not to take ourselves too seriously. Many of the best ideas come from playful experimentation.

Picasso once said his dream was to paint like a child again.

### Freedom vs Control
Unlimited freedom rarely makes a game more fun.

If players receive everything instantly, nothing feels meaningful.

Constraints, rules, and friction create tension — and tension makes achievement satisfying.

### Underdog vs World Domination
We often think of ourselves as an underdog studio. That mindset keeps us creative and hungry — but it can also limit our ambition.

We want to keep the underdog spirit while still building things that reach a large audience.

### Emotion vs Logic
Good art engages both emotion and thought.

Emotion creates connection. Logic creates meaning.

The best work does both — it makes people feel something and gives them something to think about.

---

These tensions are part of the creative process. We don't expect to solve them perfectly — we just try to navigate them thoughtfully while making things that bring people joy.

## Our Creative Test

We sometimes joke that every project must pass the **SPORK test**.

But the real question is simpler:

**“Will I want to experience this a hundred times?”**

A big part of that question is whether the **core interaction** is enjoyable on its own. If the basic action isn't fun, no amount of rewards, story, or polish will fix it. 

If the SPORK test answer is no, the audience probably won't want to play even one time.

This test has several strategic benefits:

- If repetition is enjoyable, the audience can return to the experience again and again.
- If repetition is enjoyable, creators can iterate without burning out.
- If repetition is enjoyable, practicing over and over is fun, not work.
- If repetition is enjoyable, polishing and refining is more enjoyable, so the final result is better.

## The Great Game?!

Some people consider the great game to be about emotion, making people cry. Some people say a heavy topic like historical trageties are part of the greatest work. At Silverware Games we say, if you can make a game about something boring fun, like doing laundry, that is the greatest game.
